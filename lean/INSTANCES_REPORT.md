## Full program, task 8: semantic E12 and E13

### S12: semantic individuation

`Instances/E12Semantic.lean` represents a part as a subset of a finite state space. A closure operator is extensive, monotone, and idempotent. A part is **repair-closed** when it is a fixed point of closure and every transition starting inside stays inside. It is **self-maintaining** when it is nonempty, lies inside at least one region on the system's finite boundary list, and every state inside has a successor inside. A candidate satisfies both. A candidate is **maximal** if no strictly larger candidate contains it. An **integrated individual** is maximal and has no overlapping maximal competitor. Inclusion in a declared boundary region is the chosen concrete reading of a viable boundary; it replaces the old arbitrary viability `Prop`, but does not assert that a boundary is the coarsest quotient for probes. The new semantic law does not model the older certificate's separate budget and record closure fields.

`repairClosed_inter` proves that repair-closed parts are closed under intersection for every such closure operator and transition relation. `integrated_disjoint_or_equal` proves that any two integrated individuals are equal or disjoint. In the first three-state system, states 0 and 1 repair into one another, state 2 remains separate, and the boundary list is `[{0,1}]`. The part `{0,1}` is proved integrated and is neither empty nor the whole space. In the second three-state system, transitions and closure are identity, and the boundary list is `[{0,1},{1,2}]`. Both two-state parts are maximal, overlap at 1, and `second_no_integrated` proves no integrated individual exists.

**Strengthening.** `genuineRepairClosure` saturates a healthy/damaged repair pair: if either endpoint is in a part, closure adds the other. The three-state system also has an outside state, a healthy-to-damaged perturbation relation, and an endogenous damaged-to-healthy repair relation (with stuttering allowed by its system transition). Extensivity, monotonicity and idempotence are proved. `repairedIndividualWitness` proves that the proper integrated part contains both damage and its repair target and is neither empty nor the whole state space. The healthy-only part is forward-invariant under endogenous transitions and self-maintaining, yet its closure contains the damaged state; `healthyOnly_closure_loadBearing` proves it is not a candidate. Perturbations are external challenges; the `RepairClosed` transition relation represents endogenous repair dynamics. `integrated_disjoint_or_equal` follows immediately from the no-overlapping-maximal-competitor clause in the definition; the independent intersection theorem supplies the general closure content.

The two E12 instance attempts are the earlier identity-closure component, which cannot test the closure condition, and this repair-saturation component, whose healthy-only ablation fails exactly that condition.

Two mathematical attempts: (1) transition invariance alone gives union-compatible candidates and cannot exhibit overlapping maxima; (2) an explicit boundary-viability policy permits the overlapping example while retaining a general intersection theorem. The initial finite-set implementation was unavailable in this core-only Lean library, so the final implementation uses characteristic predicates on a state type with a proved finite enumeration.

### S13: semantic repair transport

`Instances/E13Semantic.lean` defines a context-indexed repair relation, natural-number obstruction, challenge-class identifier, and state role for each system. A **paid interface** maps source states and contexts to receiver states and contexts, preserves roles and challenge class, and carries a positive natural-number payment. It is a **repair transport** when every source repair maps to a receiver repair and strictly lowers the receiver's obstruction in that same mapped class. The payment is an explicit interface cost; no conservation or optimality assertion is made.

`compose` proves that repair transports compose and that the composite payment is the sum of the two payments. Concrete source, receiver, and third systems use distinct contexts 0 and 1, both in class 7. The repair `2 → 1` is transported in each context. Obstruction is respectively `s+c`, `s+c+1`, and `s+c+2`; the paid interfaces cost 2 and 3, with composite cost 5 and strict receiver reduction. A separate paid role-preserving interface to a receiver with constant-zero obstruction maps roles, classes, and repair steps, but has no strict reduction; `roleOnly_no_reduction` proves the failure.

**Strengthening.** `RepairChain A c s t k` is a sequence of exactly `k` source repair steps from `s` to `t`. For every `RepairTransport f`, `chain_obstruction_bound` proves receiver obstruction at the mapped target plus `k` is at most receiver obstruction at the mapped start, both in mapped context `f(c)`; `chain_length_bound` gives `k` at most the starting receiver obstruction. `crossTransport` maps source context 0 to receiver context 1 and context 1 to 0, and maps each natural-number source state `s` to the distinct receiver-state type `(s,true)`. It preserves the challenge class and role, maps repairs, pays four units, and strictly decreases receiver obstruction. A two-step chain `3 → 2 → 1` in source context 0 instantiates both general bounds in receiver context 1. Payment additivity in `compose_payment` holds by construction: the composite interface's payment field is defined as the sum, while the proof that the sum remains positive uses both payments' positivity.

The two E13 instance attempts are the earlier identity-on-context transport and this context-swapping, state-type-changing transport. The latter exercises the chain theorem across the interface.
