Can a repair be credited to the system that undergoes it, rather than to an observer? This paper
states sixteen structural entries, E1--E16, for repairs whose defect, move, budget and audit are
records the system carries in its own state. It gives definitions under which such self-repair can be
checked, and proves in Lean~4 what can be proved about them; the instances are deliberately minimal.
Five entries are laws with a checked instance. A two-state device executes a repair along its own
trajectory, and a history record links the reinstatement of its apparatus to that event (E3). A
two-level audit tower checks its lower level within a declared record capacity (E2). A clocked
device installs a fresh refinement at unboundedly many repair times (E1). A three-state system has
an individual containing its own damaged state and repair (E12).
Repair transports compose, and the
receiver's obstruction bounds every chain of transported repairs (E13). The general theorems include
multiplier conditions that certify a global optimum of a capped allocation, even at a cap (E6), the
semilattice of repair joins, a finite argmax for probe acquisition and a budget-reallocation
identity. The remaining entries are certificate specifications: a complete certificate yields the
stated classification, and supplying it is the open obligation.
