\section{Certificate specifications}\label{sec:templates}

The remaining entries are certificate specifications (\Cref{sec:setting-status}), except E5 and E8,
which are definitions with consistency lemmas. Each certificate specification has a Lean statement of
the form ``a complete certificate yields the classification'': the certificate is a
carried status record asserting that one branch of the classification holds for a claim, and Lean
proves that the branches are mutually exclusive and derives the fields the branch requires.
Supplying the certificate for a real system is the open obligation. In every case the certificate
is a complete, branch-valid status record; \Cref{tab:templates} names, for each entry, the evidence
its principal branch requires. This section
prints two in full, E3 and E13, which complement the laws of \Cref{sec:concrete}. The
others are printed, with meanings and examples, in \Cref{supp:templates}, and their full field lists
are in \Cref{supp:fields}.

\subsection{E3: maintained closure}\label{sec:tmpl-e3}

\begin{schema}[E3: maintained closure]\label{tmpl:e3}
\emph{Data.} A running package with apparatus $\App_t$, a maintenance operator $\mathfrak m$ and a
reinstatement record.
\emph{Certificate.} A complete maintenance status record with four statuses: crystal-grade,
maintained, subsidized and decaying closure. The maintained branch contains an object fixed point and
record coherence, a carried maintenance operator, the reinstatement condition of
\Cref{sec:episode}, a maintained step (apparatus distance at most $\varepsilon$ without challenge, a
linked lawful repair step under challenge), an empty maintenance obstruction and no external
reinstatement witness.
\emph{Conclusion.} The four statuses are mutually exclusive, and the maintained branch yields both
object closure and apparatus reinstatement.
\end{schema}

\emph{Meaning.} A persisting object is classified by who keeps its apparatus in place.
\emph{Crystal-grade} closure is persistence with a stable apparatus and no maintenance operator or
reinstatement at all, as in a crystal; maintained closure has the system's own reinstatement;
subsidized closure has an external or fallback reinstatement; decaying closure has apparatus drift
above tolerance and no valid reinstatement. The maintenance obstruction is nonempty if a required
apparatus component is absent, has a forbidden source, fails generation or scope, is off-kernel, or
is omitted from the declared audit. The history-linked form of reinstatement
(\Cref{def:episode-linked}) is the one with a proved instance. A further Lean statement packages
measured apparatus and object distance curves after the maintenance channel is cut.
\emph{Example.} After the maintenance channel is cut, the apparatus distance runs
$0, 1/8, 3/8, 1/2, 5/8, 3/4$ and the object distance $0, 1/16, 1/16, 1/8, 3/16, 5/16$; they separate
first at time $2$. A crystal-grade control never separates.

\subsection{E13: repair transport}\label{sec:tmpl-e13}

\begin{schema}[E13: repair transport]\label{tmpl:e13}
\emph{Data.} E-systems $A$ and $B$ joined by an audited, paid bridge; a token $s$; a challenge class
$\mathcal C$ shared by source and receiver records.
\emph{Certificate.} One of four full cases, with a complete status record over coercion null,
symbolic, taught, communicated, scaffolded-or-primed, influence-only and transport-rejected:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{communication} (E13.1): $s$ induces a paid, interface-mediated, role-preserving
    carried receiver repair with a strict reduction of $B$'s obstruction in class $\mathcal C$;
  \item \emph{teaching} (E13.2): communication, and at a later probe with $A$ absent and the bridge
    closed, the repair is carried by $B$ from its own committed state;
  \item \emph{coercion null} (E13.3): a force comparator classifies the interaction as fully forced,
    a carried free-response census is empty, no $B$-committed transported repair of the token exists
    and the obstruction is unchanged;
  \item \emph{symbolic repair} (E13.4): $s$ reactivates a carried, role-preserving, strict repair in
    every context of a declared family of at least two contexts.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Conclusion.} The corresponding judgment holds, and its case records the absence of raw evidence
for every higher-priority judgment of the same claim; coercion-null evidence excludes all six lower
judgments.
\end{schema}

\emph{Meaning.} A communicated token leaves the receiver with a carried repair that strictly reduces
its obstruction in the shared class, through a paid, interface-mediated, role-preserving bridge;
teaching adds that the repair is carried by the receiver from its own committed state when the
teacher is absent and the bridge is closed. Paid, role-preserving repair transport, the core of the
communication case, is the law of \Cref{sec:e13-semantic}; its instance transports repair across two
distinct contexts of one challenge class. The full communication certificate and the teaching,
coercion-null and symbolic cases remain certificate specifications.
\emph{Example.} An interaction that a force comparator classifies as fully forced, with a carried
free-response census that is empty, no receiver-committed transported repair of the token, and an
unchanged obstruction, is a coercion null.
