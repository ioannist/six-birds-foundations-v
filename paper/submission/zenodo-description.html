Can a repair be credited to the system that undergoes it, rather than to an observer? This paper states sixteen structural entries, E1–E16, over six definitions, D1–D6, for repairs whose defect, move, budget and audit are records the system carries in its own state. It gives definitions under which such self-repair can be checked, and proves in Lean 4 what can be proved about them; the instances are deliberately minimal.

Five entries are laws with a checked instance. A two-state device executes a repair along its own trajectory, and a history record links the reinstatement of its apparatus to that event (E3). A two-level audit tower checks its lower level within a declared record capacity (E2). A clocked device installs a fresh refinement at unboundedly many repair times (E1). A three-state system has an individual containing its own damaged state and repair (E12). Repair transports compose, and the receiver's obstruction bounds every chain of transported repairs (E13).

The general theorems include multiplier conditions that certify a global optimum of a capped allocation, even at a cap (E6), the semilattice of repair joins, a finite argmax for probe acquisition and a budget-reallocation identity. The remaining entries are certificate specifications: a complete certificate yields the stated classification, and supplying it is the open obligation. Living, cognitive and social systems are candidate realizations of the same typed hypotheses; no domain realization is established here.

The supplement is a separate file. It gives the source concordance (item, entry, Lean declaration), the consistency lemmas of the definitions, the full certificate specifications and their field lists, the non-implications, the finite computations, life-facing synthetic data with a biological protocol, the formal status and axiom record, and replay instructions. The Lean 4 development and the exact-arithmetic laboratory are in the linked repository.
